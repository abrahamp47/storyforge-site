# Lark Burnhardt

A student at [[UYM Khinning]] and major antagonist/rival to [[Alexander Gloomshard]]. Mentored by the Khinning minister [[Lawrence]].

## Profile

- **Stream**: Conjury — **Wendigo** form
- **House**: Red Lotus (R) — same house as Alex
- **Family**: Burnhardt (Khinning Central); mother present at Redford station
- **Appearance**: Messy hair, hazel eyes, calm unnervingly smooth voice, accompanied by an "unnerving smile"
- **Personality**: Prideful, socially adept, initially charming. Trained to "break people and find flaws." Ego-driven but highly skilled.

## Relationship with Lawrence

Lawrence trained Lark to be "unwavering, logical... to keep emotions in check." However, Lark interpreted these lessons as instructions to create subordinates rather than allies. Their chess-game dialogue reveals:
- Lawrence assigned Lark to make an ally of "the prince" (likely Alex); Lark tried to break him instead.
- Lawrence sees Lark's ego as his critical flaw.
- Lark resents Lawrence's emotional detachment and only engaging when Lark fails tasks.

## Relationship with Alex (Novel)

Lark's arc with Alex follows a deliberate pattern:
1. **Redford Station**: Recognizes Alex as the prince (via Royce's presence). Approaches him first.
2. **Maelfrey**: Meets again; claims to be from Khinning Central and made an "educated guess."
3. **UYM Day 1**: Sends Alex a formal letter requesting friendship (following Gleadish royal protocol).
4. **Friendship Phase**: Sits beside Alex in class, helps with the Tatzelwurm incident, plays card games, goes on outings.
5. **Breaking Point**: At the Golden Ring in Harpei, Lark antagonizes Louis ("I can just ask my father to get them fired"). Alex defends Louis with his sword. Lark resents Alex's protection: "I don't need your help."
6. **Lark's Mission**: Per planning docs, Lark was assigned by Lawrence to befriend Alex and make him an ally. He interpreted this as creating a subordinate, and when Alex showed independent loyalty to Louis, the friendship fractures.

## The Prince's Challenge

Lark is publicly challenged to combat by a prince figure. Lawrence warns this is a miscalculation — not of combat ability but of political strategy. Despite "great prowess, technique, intelligence," their larger scheme has failed.
